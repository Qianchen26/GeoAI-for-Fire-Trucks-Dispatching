# Supplementary figure captions

**Figure S1. E1 differences in spatial fire-travel dispersion.** Circles and 95% intervals use 2,000 paired bootstrap samples of 15 whole event streams (seeds 101–115); diamonds show the saved fast-evaluator differences. The six scenario–year checks pair Medium trend (w = 1), High trend (w = 1), and High proportional growth (w = 0) with 2035 and 2045. D_F is the fixed-fire-rate-weighted standard deviation of tract mean fire travel; M_F and M_O are incident-level regional means for study-fire and other calls. Travel is en route to arrival. Negative differences favor PPO. The two trend configurations match in 2035; High proportional 2035 uses a different vehicle mix. All four nonidentical simulator dispersion intervals cross zero. Event-stream intervals describe variation within each scenario–year and exclude evaluator discrepancy.

**Figure S2. Local fire-travel consequences: PPO versus no action.** The 2045 Medium scenario uses historical-trend population allocation (w = 1). Differences compare frozen PPO with no action, using saved fast-evaluator tract means. Travel is first-arriving eligible vehicle travel from en route to arrival. Negative values favor PPO. The symmetric color range is ±160 s, shared with the other scenarios for this comparison. Annotations sum fixed baseline fire-rate weights in faster, slower, and unchanged tracts; differences within 10⁻¹⁰ min count as numerical equality. These categories use point estimates. No action retains the initial 25 locations and 28 vehicles.

**Figure S3. Policy contrasts across 48 held-out growth draws.** Squares show fast-evaluator differences in the mean of four evaluation years (2030, 2035, 2040, 2045), averaged over 48 held-out growth draws. Intervals are the existing 95% paired bootstrap intervals over whole draws. D_F is the fixed-fire-rate-weighted standard deviation of tract mean fire travel; M_F and M_O are incident-level regional means for study-fire and other calls. Travel is en route to arrival. Negative values favor PPO on the plotted metric. PPO has a larger D_F than stage greedy by 0.251 s (95% CI 0.164–0.332 s). These intervals describe growth-draw variation, distinct from the event-stream variation in E1.

**Figure S4. Spatial weights for study-fire demand.** Each tract contributes its share of the fixed study-fire rate estimated from October 2024–March 2025 incidents. Study-fire calls comprise outside, structure, vehicle, and brush fires; other calls form the remaining modeled call group. These shares are county demand weights, rather than per-resident risk rates. One of the 58 tracts has zero baseline fire rate. The scenarios retain these fire rates while non-fire workload changes with population. Triangles show the initial modeled station/base locations.

**Figure S5. Baseline service under the 2030 Medium trend scenario.** The fast evaluator estimates first-arriving eligible engine/quint travel with the unchanged 2025 configuration and 2030 Medium historical-trend growth (w = 1). Travel is en route to arrival. The hatched tract has zero baseline fire rate and an undefined fire-travel mean. Triangles show the initial modeled station/base locations. The scenario keeps fire rates fixed and changes non-fire workload with population.
