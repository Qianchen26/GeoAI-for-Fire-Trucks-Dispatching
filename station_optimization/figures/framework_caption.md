**Figure 2. Framework for staged fire-resource planning.** Spatial demand, the current station and vehicle configuration, candidate sites, and stage constraints form the planning state. The actor trained by proximal policy optimization (PPO) maps 579 state values through two 256-unit tanh layers to 659 action scores. Action masks restrict the available station and vehicle decisions. Each stage updates the resource configuration and passes the resulting state to the next stage. During training, the approximate spatial queueing model supplies rewards under growth scenarios and protocol dispatch; the dashed arrow denotes this training feedback. The study evaluated frozen policies with discrete-event simulation: E1 compared PPO with stage greedy, and E2 evaluated two engine-addition alternatives under frozen-policy continuation. Decisions occurred in 2025, 2030, 2035, and 2040, with evaluations five years later. Outcomes describe variation in fire travel across census tracts, mean travel times, and the locations of gains or losses. The neural nodes are schematic. The critic used during PPO training is omitted.

Travel time is measured from en route to arrival. The figure specifies the architecture and workflow; subsequent figures report policy and action comparisons.
